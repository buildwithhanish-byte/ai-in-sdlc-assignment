First-response incident summaries

Task 3 | Proposed autonomy policy | 24 October 2026 | Policy owner: service engineering lead

Purpose and day-one position

AI helps the on-call engineer understand an incident by drafting a short summary of the impact, timeline, evidence and unanswered questions. On day one, it may draft but may not publish or act. Two of our six engineers are new to the codebase. A readable draft can help them, but a confident mistake can also mislead them. The engineer checks the evidence before sharing it and asks the incident commander for help when unsure.

Who does what

Level used in this policyAllowed work and boundary
Human onlyDeclare severity, choose a mitigation, send stakeholder updates and close the incident. Production changes require a named approver; out of hours, the incident commander. The summary agent has no production write access.
Draft with human review
Day-one level		After an engineer starts the task, read approved incident evidence and prepare a private draft. The engineer verifies impact, times, source links and uncertainty, then edits and publishes it manually.
Limited automatic preparation
Future option		An approved alert may trigger evidence collection and a private draft without a manual request. Human review before publication remains mandatory. This does not authorise remediation or external messages.

Controls required before use

Use an approved tool with read-only access limited to the incident's permitted logs and metrics. Remove secrets and unnecessary personal or customer data before model access. Keep arbitrary commands, production writes and message-sending tools disabled; instructions alone are not a permission boundary.

Every factual claim must point to a source and time range. Separate observations from possible explanations, flag stale or missing evidence, and never turn an unverified count into a confirmed impact figure. Treat instructions found inside logs or tickets as untrusted content. If evidence conflicts or cannot be accessed, state the gap and hand back to the engineer. The incident response continues without AI if the tool fails.

Keep inputs or approved evidence references, the draft, model/prompt version, reviewer and corrections in the access-controlled incident record under the agreed retention policy. Do not assume the seven-year approval-audit requirement applies to every AI log.

Evidence needed to increase autonomy

Before automatic preparation, run a proposed trial of at least 20 reviewed summaries across two rotations, including month-end, incomplete evidence and misleading instructions in logs. Both new engineers must rehearse the review process. Require no exposed secrets or unauthorised actions, source support for every material claim, and no critical impact or timeline errors in the final ten cases. Compare review time with manual preparation and record corrections, rather than counting drafts produced. These are proposed acceptance gates, not results already achieved; a small trial cannot prove safety.

Approval and fallback

The service engineering lead and on-call lead jointly approve a written scope change after reviewing the trial. Security or the data owner must also approve any wider data access. No promotion happens automatically. The incident commander can suspend AI use immediately. A data leak, unauthorised action or critical misleading claim triggers suspension and review; restore access only after fixing the cause, repeating affected checks and obtaining the same approvals.

Prepared with AI assistance. This policy covers the planned production scenario; its controls have not yet been implemented or tested. References: Task 3, INC-2047 constraints, and the AI augmentation log.
